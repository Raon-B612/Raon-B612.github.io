Bridges Through Art

Creating connections across generations, one creative activity at a time.

A youth-led toolkit for meaningful intergenerational engagement, inspired by our time with older adults in senior living communities.

Explore the toolkit Read our story

How it started

A group of young people walked into senior living communities with art supplies, activity ideas, and a wish to connect with older adults.

Across the sessions, we found the activities were about more than making art. They gave us reasons to talk, share memories, trade perspectives, and learn from each other. Those relationships became the foundation for this toolkit.

Start your own program

You don't need experience to begin. These are the steps we followed, from finding a partner to reflecting afterwards.

  1. Find a community partnerContact a local senior living community's activities director.
  2. Recruit youth participantsFriends, classmates, clubs, or a youth group.
  3. Choose an activityPick one from the activity guides below.
  4. Prepare materialsBring extras, and plan for different abilities.
  5. Meet your partnersIntroductions first; the craft can wait.
  6. Create togetherWork side by side, not for someone.
  7. Reflect togetherWhat did you learn from each other?

Activity guides

Each guide is ready to use: what to bring, how to run it, and questions that get conversations going.

Collaborative memory collage

Time: 45–60 minutes Group: Youth and older adults Difficulty: Beginner
You'll need
  • Magazines
  • Colored paper
  • Scissors (include easy-grip pairs)
  • Glue sticks
  • Markers
Before the session
  • Pre-sort magazine images into a few themes so choosing is easier.
  • Set up tables so youth and older adults sit next to each other.
During the session
  1. Introduce yourself and the activity.
  2. Invite participants to choose images.
  3. Encourage youth and older adults to work together on one collage.
  4. Use the conversation starters below.
  5. Give everyone time to share their creations.
Conversation starters

What does this image remind you of?

What was something you enjoyed doing when you were young?

Is there a tradition that is important to you?

After the session

Ask the youth: what did you learn from the person you worked with?

Youth guide

Don't just lead the activity. Build the relationship.

Do

  • Learn about the community before you visit.
  • Prepare flexible activities.
  • Bring extra materials.
  • Think about accessibility.
  • Prepare conversation questions.

Don't

  • Assume what older adults can or can't do.
  • Treat participants as passive recipients.
  • Focus only on finishing the craft.
  • Assume everyone has the same interests or abilities.
